The stone money of Yap

On the island of Yap, money was carved from stone: giant discs too heavy to ever move. So people kept track of value together, by trust, memory and behavior. One stone sank to the bottom of the sea, and it still counted.

Nobel economist Milton Friedman later used Yap to show that all money rests on shared belief. He told the story in The Island of Stone Money (1991).

The stone was never the money.
The behavior was.

The four dimensions of your pattern

Self-discovery across every layer of your financial life.

Saving & Spending

How you spend and save

Do you save instinctively — or spend freely and save what's left? Your saving pattern reveals the emotional relationship you have with money, and whether you build by habit, by willpower, or have blocks in the way.

Retirement

How you plan for your future

Do you imagine retirement vividly — or does the future feel abstract and easy to defer? Your planning pattern reveals how connected you are to your future self.

Investing

How you invest

Bold, cautious, or conflicted? Your investment pattern reveals how much uncertainty you're comfortable with — and whether you tilt toward loss-aversion, regret, or confidence in your own judgement.

Market Response

How you react under pressure

When markets fall, do you buy, hold, or panic? Your market-response pattern is one of the most powerful predictors of your outcomes — and reveals whether your wiring works for you or against you.
